### Summary of the artefact

This news article by Prothero (2026) is an edited conversation about the potential ethical dilemmas in teachers using AI in classes. The interviewed argues that, whilst AI saves teachers time, concerns have been raised, including students arguing the hypocrisy and false positive results from AI detectors, impacting trust between students and teachers.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this article because it focused on a common discussion about AI use affecting multiple professional fields. This particular artefact aligns with the workshop discussion highlighting how AI detectors on students’ work provide ethical dilemmas. From my perspective, supported by the article, the use of AI is an ethical issue in that it can become hypocritical for teachers to use, and has been shown to be inaccurate. I feel the article provides me more knowledge and perspective in the debate about AI in classrooms for both students and teachers.

## Artefact 3:  Journal Article

<img width="880" height="716" alt="AI paper" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/49567546-9ce0-4bad-bbf7-7b45a34f654a" />
https://academic.oup.com/nsr/article/12/5/nwaf050/8029900

### Summary of the artefact

This journal article by He et al (2025) breaks down the history and ongoing developments of generative AI through breaking down the technology to four categories. Rules based generative systems, Model-based generative algorithms, Deep generative methodologies and foundation models. It also explores the potential limitations of each component of the technology.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this journal article because it provides a more extensive history of the generative AI service most people think about today. The workshop discussion summarises the history of AI use as a broad subject. The four categories of the technology not only makes learning the history of generative AI easier to comprehend, but it also aids in learning how and why AI functions.
